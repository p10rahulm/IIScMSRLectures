<b>Abstract:</b>
In this three-lecture mini-course we will cover two fundamental concepts in computational geometry: randomized incremental construction and arrangements.  Randomized incremental construction (RIC), and lazy RIC, are simple and elegant, yet extremely powerful, design technique for geometric algorithms. Arrangements, which arise in motion planning, visibility problems, and many other applications and they play a key role in designing and analyzing algorithm for these application.  I will discuss some beautiful tools that are used in the analysis of arrangements,including Davenport–Schinzel sequences  and the Clarkson–Shor technique, and show several applications. Along the way, we will visit some basic geometric structures such as Voronoi diagrams and Delauney triangulations. The mini-course does not require a background in computational geometry.
